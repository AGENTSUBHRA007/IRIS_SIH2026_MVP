📚 3. Defect Classification Guide & Hazard Mitigation
⚡
CRACK (Cover Surface Crack)
Surface Degradation • ISO 15236-2 (Surface Quality & Cover Cracking Limits)

What is it? A crack or fracture line in the top rubber protective layer. Caused by rubber aging, flex fatigue, or sun exposure.

Why is it dangerous? If ignored, rainwater and fine dust seep inside and rust the steel cords or rot fabric plies, causing sudden belt break.

Recommended Operator Action: Monitor during next routine walkaround. Apply cold vulcanizing rubber patch or sealant during scheduled stoppage.

💥
TEAR (Longitudinal / Through Tear)
Structural Integrity • ISO 15236-4 (Longitudinal Rip Resistance & DIN 22101 Tension Integrity)

What is it? A deep cut or rip piercing through the conveyor belt. Usually caused by sharp rock edges or trapped metal at the transfer chute.

Why is it dangerous? CRITICAL: The tear can rapidly spread along the entire conveyor length within minutes, causing catastrophic belt splitting and massive downtime.

Recommended Operator Action: Slow conveyor immediately. Inspect transfer chute. Install emergency mechanical fastener rip-stop clips or hot vulcanized repair patch.

🕳️
SURFACE DAMAGE (Cover Gouge / Impact Crater)
Cover Integrity • ISO 15236-2 (Impact Damage & Cover Thickness Retention)

What is it? A chunk or gouge of rubber torn off the carrying surface from heavy rock drops at the feed chute.

Why is it dangerous? Reduces carrying capacity and allows ore fines to pack into the gouge, causing further rubber tearing under idler rollers.

Recommended Operator Action: Clean debris from crater and fill with fast-curing polyurethane repair compound during lunch stoppage.

🔗
SPLICE GAP (Joint Splice Gap / Separation)
Joint Integrity • DIN 22129 / ISO 15236-3 (Static & Dynamic Splice Strength)

What is it? Opening or stretching at the belt connection joint (splice). The glue/vulcanized bond between belt ends is pulling apart.

Why is it dangerous? VERY HIGH: A failing splice can snap completely under full ore load, dropping the belt into the conveyor structure.

Recommended Operator Action: Inspect joint step separation immediately with caliper. Schedule emergency re-splicing if gap > 3.0 mm.

✂️
EDGE DAMAGE (Belt Edge Fray / Sidewall Wear)
Tracking & Idler Damage • IS 11592 / DIN 22101 (Belt Alignment & Edge Clearance)

What is it? Frayed or shredded edges caused by the belt rubbing against conveyor steel frames or misaligned guide rollers.

Why is it dangerous? Weakens the lateral stiffness of the belt, causes severe mistracking, and risks steel cords peeling out from the sides.

Recommended Operator Action: Re-align training idlers and adjust belt tracking sensor limit switches. Trim frayed cords.

⚠️
FOREIGN OBJECT (Trapped Foreign Object / Scrap)
Chute & Carry Hazard • IS 11592 Safety Regulations & Chute Clearance

What is it? Unwanted metal piece (tramp iron), wedged boulder, or scraper tool stuck on the belt or in the skirtboard.

Why is it dangerous? Acts like a knife, scoring or slicing the entire length of the conveyor belt as it runs.

Recommended Operator Action: Stop feed chute, safely lock out conveyor, and manually remove the trapped foreign object immediately.
